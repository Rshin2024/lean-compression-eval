What the metrics and adjustments mean

T₀ compares expanded reference counts to statement-and-proof source length. I₀ compares proof length to statement length; higher I₀ favors a longer proof for a fixed statement. These implement the paper’s §5 ratios using this evaluation’s frozen Lean measurement rules. Proof concision ranks WB in the opposite direction: shorter first.

Within a question, take the geometric mean across usable samples. Average question rank gives every shared question equal weight, with rank 1 for the largest T₀, I₀ or J₀, or the smallest WB. Ties share the occupied ranks. Aggregate metric value uses an equal-question geometric mean for T₀, I₀ and WB, and an arithmetic mean for J₀. The paper defines element-level measures; these model-level aggregation choices are ours.

For J₀, normalize each question’s geometric T₀ and I₀ by their respective maximum among compared models, then apply β. The paper leaves normalization open; our maximum-based choice is exploratory and depends on the compared models and usable samples. Aggregate scores also depend on the selected questions. J₀ uses floating-point calculations and a 10⁻¹² tie tolerance. T₀, I₀ and WB ordering uses exact integer products and powers; displayed values are rounded.

PageRank I₁ and its α parameter are not available in this report. They require a separately specified dependency-graph population and graph analysis. The primitive boundary and library are frozen measurement choices, not display controls.

Failures have no invented compression values. These rankings are conditional on usable valid proofs; success counts retain all scheduled attempts. Increasing the sample requirement can reduce the shared question set. No confidence intervals or reliable-winner claims are attached. Library expansion can inflate T₀; high I₀ is not evidence of a concise proof.
