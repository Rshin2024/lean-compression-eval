How the correlations are calculated

All ranking filters above apply to the metric, cohort and shared questions. Validity retains every scheduled slot for those models, questions and selected sample indices, including failed and truncated attempts. A proof-length filter restricts compression measurements only; it never removes failures or longer valid proofs from the validity denominator.

At model level, correlate the displayed aggregate metric with accepted attempts divided by scheduled attempts on the same shared questions. At question level, use one observation per model and question. Rank points rescale the existing average question rank to 0–100, with larger values meaning a better position for the chosen objective. Magnitude uses log₂ T₀, log₂ I₀, raw normalized J₀, or log₂ proof-body length. Larger Wᴮ magnitude means longer proofs; larger Wᴮ rank points mean shorter proofs.

Pearson r measures linear association on the displayed axes. Spearman ρ measures rank association, with ties receiving midranks. Metric order uses exact ratio evidence where available. Correlations need at least three points and variation in both variables. A constant success rate produces an unavailable correlation, not zero.

The question-level within-question r subtracts the question’s mean x and mean success rate from every point before calculating Pearson r. This removes between-question mean differences only. It does not remove model differences or selection bias. Repeated model–question observations are not independent; these descriptive correlations have no p-values or confidence intervals.

A higher minimum-success requirement selects easier or more consistently solved model–question groups. Selecting only one sample, or requiring all samples to be usable, may force every included success rate to 100%. The same outputs supply the compression and validity summaries; these are not held-out prediction tests.
